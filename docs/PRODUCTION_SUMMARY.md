## 1. The film

| | |
|---|---|
| Title | BEHIND TWO WINDOWS |
| Runtime | 2 min 47 s (167.4 s) |
| Format | MP4 — H.264 video + AAC audio |
| Resolution | 1920 × 1080 (16:9) |
| Frame rate | 24 fps (cinematic) |
| Sequences | 10, cross-dissolved |
| Frames rendered | 4,104 |
| Language | English |

**Story.** Aarav and Maya are both eleven, both live in the same Indian city, and both want
to be scientists. Aarav has a laptop, a wall of books and a parent who brings him more. Maya
has the same curiosity and builds a working solar tracker out of bottles and cardboard. Their
paths cross at a community science exhibition; they build together; and the community turns
that into an inclusive learning centre open to every child.

**The rule we set ourselves.** Inequality is shown only as unequal *access* — a queue for one
shared computer, a shelf with three books, a borrowed table. Maya is never portrayed as
helpless, and poverty is never used as a costume.

## 2. Tools used — and what each one cost

Every tool in this production is **free and open source**. No subscription, credit pack or
paid upgrade was bought, and **no asset was downloaded or purchased** — every character,
prop, building, camera move, musical note and sound effect in the film was generated by the
code in this project.

| Tool | Version | Role in the production | Licence / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blender** (`bpy` Python module) | 5.3 | All 3D work: character rigs, sets, props, cameras, animation, rendering — driven headlessly from Python, never through the GUI | Free · GPL |
| **Blender Workbench** engine | — | The renderer. Studio lighting + shadows + cavity shading gave the clay look at ~2.6 s/frame on a 2-core CPU | Free, ships with Blender |
| **Python** | 3.13 | Scene construction, animation maths, audio synthesis, build scripts | Free · PSF |
| **NumPy / SciPy** | — | Frame buffers, filters, the whole music and sound-effects engine | Free · BSD |
| **Kokoro-82M** via `kokoro-onnx` | v1.0 | The narration voice — neural text-to-speech run fully offline through ONNX Runtime | Free · Apache-2.0 |
| **FFmpeg** | 6.1 | Sky compositing, colour grade, bloom, cross-dissolves, title overlays, audio ducking, final H.264/AAC export | Free · LGPL |
| **Pillow (PIL)** | — | Sky gradients, title cards, character reference sheets, contact sheets | Free · HPND |
| **pptxgenjs** | — | The accompanying presentation | Free · MIT |
| **Claude** (AI assistant) | — | Pair-programmer: concept, code, reviewing rendered frames, fixing staging | Included in the user's plan |

### Tools considered and rejected

| Tool | Why not |
|---|---|
| Cycles (Blender path tracer) | 9.4 s/frame at 720p on this CPU → ~11 hours for the film, with no GPU available |
| EEVEE / EEVEE Next | Requires a GPU; the container exposes none (EGL initialisation fails) |
| Online text-to-video / image-to-video generators | Cannot hold a character's face, clothing and proportions steady across 10 shots, which the brief explicitly forbids |
| Stock 3D model libraries | Unnecessary — everything needed was cheaper to write as a parametric function |

## 3. How the film was made

```
1  Script      10 narration lines written to the SDG 10 brief
2  Voice       Kokoro-82M neural TTS, offline → 10 WAV files + measured lengths
3  Timeline    shot lengths derived from the voice lengths (10 shots = 2:47)
4  3D build    Blender bpy: rigs, sets, props, cameras — all from code
5  Render      Workbench engine, transparent film, 1280×720 @ 24 fps
6  Composite   sky, haze, bloom, grade, vignette, grain → upscaled to 1080p
7  Mix & edit  cross-dissolves, title cards, score + SFX ducked under narration
```

**Character consistency.** Aarav and Maya are produced by a single parametric constructor, so
every shot instantiates identical geometry, colours and proportions. A 13-joint puppet
hierarchy (root → pelvis → spine → neck → head, plus two arms and two legs) is posed by
keyframing euler angles. Character drift is impossible by construction.

**Level of detail.** The first version of the city gave every building its own windows:
~25,000 objects and **47 s per frame** — over five hours for one shot. Three LOD levels
(individual windows / per-floor strips / plain blocks) brought it down to **2.4 s per frame**
with no visible difference at that camera distance.

**The look.** The 3D is rendered over a transparent film; the sky, atmospheric haze, bloom,
colour grade, vignette and grain are composited afterwards in FFmpeg. Nine per-sequence
"moods" carry the colour arc of the story — warm dawn, bright home, cooler modest home,
dream, neutral contrast, exhibition hall, golden, bright, sunset.

## 4. Audio

| Element | How it was made |
|---|---|
| **Narration** | Kokoro-82M, voice `af_heart` at 0.90 speed. 10 lines, 75.6 s of voice inside a 167 s film |
| **Score** | Written and synthesised from scratch in NumPy — no samples, no loops, no library. Felt piano (inharmonic partials + hammer noise), string ensemble (5-way detune + vibrato), warm pad, harp, timpani, convolution reverb built from shaped noise |
| **Effects** | Morning birds (FM chirps), city and room tone, footsteps, pencil on paper, paper rustle, a servo for the solar tracker, crowd murmur from banded noise bursts, children laughing, and applause assembled from ~230 individual claps |
| **Mix** | Narration on top; score and effects side-chain compressed against the voice so they duck when the narrator speaks and swell in the gaps. Limiter at −0.4 dB |

Musical arc: soft piano (0:00) → strings enter (0:50) → yearning and sparse (1:03) →
hope returns (1:19) → building (1:37) → full crescendo (2:11) → gentle piano close (2:28).

## 6. What was difficult

1. **No GPU.** EEVEE could not initialise and Cycles was far too slow, so the whole look was
   designed around the Workbench rasteriser with the atmosphere added in post.
2. **Camera mathematics.** The first "aim at" function pointed the camera backwards — every
   early test frame was an empty field. Fixed by deriving the euler angles from the look
   vector properly (`rz = atan2(−dx, dy)`, `rx = atan2(hypot(dx, dy), −dz)`).
3. **Staging.** Characters initially faced away from their own desks, because a character's
   face points along −Y at zero rotation. Once the convention was fixed, all ten shots were
   re-staged.
4. **Render budget.** 4,104 frames on two CPU cores. Solved with level-of-detail geometry,
   anti-aliasing tuned to the point where added grain hides the difference, and rendering at
   720p with the final upscale handled in the composite.
